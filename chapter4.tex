\chapter{RESULTS AND DISCUSSION}
%{\kill{\section{ffff}}}
{\baselineskip=1.2\baselineskip


This chapter presents the results and discussion of the testing and evaluation conducted on the developed Solar-Powered Rental Station with Detachable Power Sources. The findings are organized into two major components: mobile application testing, and hardware testing. System testing focuses on the overall functionality and integration of the system, including rental transactions, data processing, and communication between components. Mobile application testing evaluates the performance, usability, and responsiveness of the user interface, including account management, rental requests, and real-time monitoring features. Hardware testing examines the operation of key physical components.


The results obtained from these tests are analyzed in relation to the objectives of the study to determine the system’s effectiveness, reliability, and efficiency in providing a sustainable and accessible power solution. Furthermore, this chapter discusses the system’s strengths, identifies encountered issues, and highlights areas for improvement based on the observed performance during testing.


}






\section {Hardware Testing Results and Discussion}


\subsection{Hardware Design}


This section presents the actual hardware implementation of the detachable power source and the solar-powered rental station. It provides a clear representation of the system’s physical structure, dimensions, and component arrangement. The figures illustrate how the developed system is assembled and how each part is positioned, allowing a better understanding of the system’s real-world configuration and usability.

\subsubsection{Component Layout and System Arrangement}

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \caption{Solar-Powered Rental Station Internal Component Layout}
    \label{fig:3d}
    \includegraphics[width=1\textwidth]{figures/station-design.png}
\end{figure}


The figure shows the structural and functional layout of the solar-powered rental station, which serves as the main hub for energy generation, storage, and distribution. The components are arranged in a way that supports efficient power management while keeping the system accessible and easy to operate. The solar charge controller is positioned on the upper panel to regulate the energy collected from the photovoltaic system and to prevent overcharging of the LiFePO4 battery located at the lower section of the enclosure. Placing the battery at the base helps maintain stability due to its weight and also supports safer heat dissipation.

Near the controller, the DC/AC breakers are organized in a dedicated panel to allow easy access and provide protection against electrical faults. The power inverter and battery charger are placed on the working platform to enable efficient conversion of DC to AC power and controlled charging of the detachable units. For user interaction, the coin slot mechanism is centrally located at the front, while the LCD is positioned at eye level to make system information easy to read. Meanwhile, the ESP32 and buck converter are housed in a side compartment to protect sensitive components while keeping them close enough for accurate monitoring and control. Overall, the layout follows a structured and layered design, separating power, control, and user interface components to improve safety, ease of maintenance, and overall system performance.

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \caption{Detachable Power Source Internal Component Layout}
    \label{fig:3d}
    \includegraphics[width=1\textwidth]{figures/portable-design.jpg}
\end{figure}


The figure presents the internal configuration of the portable detachable power source, which is designed to be compact, mobile, and reliable during use. At the center of the enclosure is the LiFePO4 battery, occupying the largest space to maintain balance and ensure stability when the unit is being transported. The remaining components are arranged along the inner walls to make efficient use of space while keeping the system organized.

On one side, the ESP32 microcontroller and voltage divider are installed to allow real-time monitoring of battery conditions while keeping the wiring simple and manageable. Directly below them, the buck converter regulates the voltage supplied to low-power components. On the opposite side, the relay module is positioned near the power inverter to control the output supplied to connected devices. The DC breaker is placed near the top section for quick disconnection in case of faults, adding an extra layer of safety. A compact Wi-Fi router is also included to support communication between the unit and the monitoring system.

The overall layout highlights a modular and accessible design, making it easier to maintain or replace components when needed. Proper spacing between heat-generating and sensitive parts is also considered to support better thermal management and extend component lifespan. In general, the portable unit demonstrates a well-organized internal structure that balances functionality, durability, and ease of use in actual operating conditions.

The solar-powered rental station is designed as a freestanding, roofed structure that integrates energy generation, storage, and user interaction in a single unit. It features a dual-sloped roof fitted with photovoltaic panels to maximize solar energy harvesting, which is then stored in internal LiFePO4 batteries and converted into usable AC power through an inverter. The overall structure stands approximately 7 to 8 feet tall, supported by vertical posts that elevate the main enclosure for protection and accessibility.

The station is designed with a ground clearance of approximately 76 cm from the base to the main enclosure. This elevation helps protect the internal components from potential ground-level hazards such as flooding, dirt accumulation, and accidental contact, while also improving ventilation and ease of maintenance. The raised design further enhances user ergonomics by positioning the access points at a comfortable height.

The central cabinet, measuring about 89 cm in width and 61 cm in height, houses essential components such as the ESP32 microcontroller, coin-slot mechanism, solenoid locking system, and monitoring interfaces. This enclosure also contains designated compartments for securely storing detachable power units, which are released only after successful payment verification. Overall, the design prioritizes durability, weather resistance, and efficient system integration, making it suitable for deployment in community areas experiencing frequent power interruptions.

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \caption{Detachable Power Source – Hardware Design Dimensions (Front View)}
    \label{fig:3d2}
    \includegraphics[width=1\textwidth]{figures/portable1.png}
\end{figure}


The portable power source is a compact, detachable unit designed to provide temporary and mobile electricity for users. With approximate dimensions of 30 cm in width and 33 cm in height, the device is structured for easy handling while maintaining sufficient capacity to power small-to-medium appliances. Its front panel includes an LCD display for monitoring battery status, indicator lights, control buttons, and multiple AC output ports for device connectivity. 

Internally, the unit contains a LiFePO4 battery, inverter, and battery management system, enabling safe energy storage and DC-to-AC conversion. A GPS and GSM module, typically positioned at the top section, allows real-time tracking and communication with the system for security and monitoring purposes. As part of the rental system, this portable unit serves as the user’s primary power supply during outages and is designed to be returned after use, with remaining battery capacity contributing to user credit within the mobile application.


\section {Mobile Application Testing Results}


\subsection {Graphical User Interface Implementation}
This section presents the graphical user interface (GUI) of the KuRyenta mobile application and administrative dashboard. The results show a fully functional interface capable of managing hardware-software interactions in real-time.

The left panel shows a splash screen with a “Get Started” control; the right panel shows a gateway card offering Log In and Sign Up. This screen functions as the access point to the authentication flow.
 The successful implementation of the splash screen and the gateway card demonstrates a clear separation between branding and the functional authentication flow, facilitating ease of use for first-time community residents.


 \begin{figure}[H]

Registration is implemented as a three-step process. Step 1 captures account credentials (username, email, phone number, password, and confirmation). Step 2 records profile and address data (sex, barangay, municipality, province, and birthdate). Step 3 presents a data-privacy notice and obtains consent to the Terms of Service and Privacy Policy before account creation.
 The data privacy notice serves as a critical checkpoint for obtaining informed consent before account activation.

The resident home dashboard effectively centralizes user data. The greeting header and location metadata provide immediate context, while the points balance card allows users to check their rental capability at a glance. Testing showed that the bottom navigation bar successfully provides persistent access to all core application modules.


\begin{figure}[H]
\centering

\label{fig:renting}
\includegraphics[width=1\textwidth]{figures/SE5.png}

The renting module allows search by Station ID and shows slot availability per station. Users select a slot and view its battery percentage with the corresponding price. Payment options include Pay via Points and Pay via Coin Slot, enabling transaction initiation.


\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\caption{Pay via Coin Slot (Community Resident)}
\label{fig:coin-slot}
\includegraphics[width=1\textwidth]{figures/coin-slot.png}
\end{figure}


The Pay via Coin Slot interface enables users to initiate the rental of a detachable power source through a physical payment mechanism. After selecting a station and an available slot, the system displays the battery percentage and corresponding rental price. The user proceeds to the station and inserts coins into the coin slot, which is detected by the system and validated. Once sufficient payment is confirmed, the system activates the solenoid lock to release the selected power source. This functionality supports accessibility for users without digital payment methods and aligns with the system’s coin-operated rental mechanism.


\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\caption{Pay via Credit Points (Community Resident)}
\label{fig:credit-points}
\includegraphics[width=1\textwidth]{figures/credit-points.png}
\end{figure}


The Pay via Credit Points interface allows users to rent a detachable power source using stored digital credits within the mobile application. After selecting a station and slot, the system calculates the rental cost based on the battery percentage of the unit. The required amount is deducted from the user’s available credits upon confirmation. Once payment is successfully processed, the system authorizes the release of the power source. This feature provides a convenient, cashless alternative and supports the system’s credit-based transaction capability.

This functionality ensures that users remain informed about the status of their rented power source and encourages timely actions, such as returning the unit or conserving energy. It enhances user awareness, system reliability, and overall operational efficiency.

This feature supports user verification, account management, and transparency by providing access to essential personal and account-related information within the KuRyenta system.
