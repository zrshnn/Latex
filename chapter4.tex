This chapter presents the results and discussion of the testing and evaluation conducted on the developed Solar-Powered Rental Station with Detachable Power Sources. The findings are organized into two major components: mobile application testing, and hardware testing. System testing focuses on the overall functionality and integration of the system, including rental transactions, data processing, and communication between components. Mobile application testing evaluates the performance, usability, and responsiveness of the user interface, including account management, rental requests, and real-time monitoring features. Hardware testing examines the operation of key physical components.


The results obtained from these tests are analyzed in relation to the objectives of the study to determine the system’s effectiveness, reliability, and efficiency in providing a sustainable and accessible power solution. Furthermore, this chapter discusses the system’s strengths, identifies encountered issues, and highlights areas for improvement based on the observed performance during testing.

\section {Solar Energy Harvesting}

The solar energy harvesting component of the system functions as the primary source of power generation. Photovoltaic (PV) panels capture solar radiation and convert it into direct current (DC) electricity through the photovoltaic effect. This generated energy is then regulated by a solar charge controller to ensure safe and efficient charging of the LiFePO4 battery, preventing overcharging and voltage fluctuations. Protective components such as DC breakers are integrated to safeguard the system against electrical faults.

The effectiveness of solar energy harvesting was observed to be dependent on environmental conditions, particularly solar irradiance and weather patterns. Under optimal sunlight conditions, the system consistently generated sufficient energy to charge the station battery and support the charging of detachable power sources. This confirms that the integration of photovoltaic technology provides a sustainable and reliable off-grid energy solution for communities experiencing frequent power interruptions .

\newpage

\section {Battery DC Power Converted to AC Output via Inverter}

The stored DC power in the LiFePO4 battery is converted into alternating current (AC) using a pure sine wave inverter. This conversion is necessary to supply electricity compatible with common household appliances, which typically operate on AC power. The inverter ensures stable voltage output and waveform quality, enabling safe operation of small-to-medium scale electrical devices.

Results show that the inverter successfully delivered usable AC power with minimal energy loss, maintaining consistent output during operation. This demonstrates that the system is capable of supporting practical household applications, thereby enhancing its usability as a temporary backup power source. The integration of the inverter is critical in bridging the gap between renewable energy storage and real-world appliance compatibility .

\newpage

\section {ESP32 Measures Battery Voltage and Displays Data on LCD}

The ESP32 microcontroller plays a vital role in monitoring system parameters, particularly battery voltage levels. It continuously reads voltage data from the battery and processes this information for real-time display on the LCD interface. This allows users and administrators to monitor the current energy status of the system.

The implementation demonstrated accurate and responsive voltage readings, which were effectively displayed on the LCD. This feature enhances system transparency and usability by providing immediate feedback on battery conditions, enabling users to make informed decisions regarding power usage and rental availability.

\newpage

\section {ESP32 Receives Location Data From GPS Satellites Using WiFi}

The system integrates GPS and communication modules to enable location tracking of detachable power sources. The GPS module collects real-time geographic coordinates from satellites, while the ESP32 processes this data and transmits it via network connectivity (WiFi/GSM) to the system database and mobile application.

Testing confirmed that the system can reliably acquire and transmit location data, allowing both users and administrators to monitor the position of rented units. This feature significantly enhances system security and accountability, ensuring that portable power sources are trackable throughout the rental duration .

\newpage

\section {Battery Monitoring and Power Calculation}

Battery monitoring is implemented through continuous measurement of voltage, usage duration, and remaining capacity. The system calculates the available power based on battery percentage and tracks energy consumption during rental use. This data is used to determine system performance and efficiency.

The results indicate that the monitoring system provides accurate estimations of battery status, enabling effective management of energy resources. Additionally, the calculated unused power serves as a basis for credit computation, supporting the system’s reward mechanism and promoting efficient energy usage.

\newpage

\section {Rental Time Tracking and Rule-Based Control}

The system incorporates a rule-based control mechanism to manage rental duration and enforce usage policies. Each rental session is limited to a predefined time (e.g., 12 hours), which is tracked by the ESP32. Automated notifications are sent to users prior to expiration, and repeated alerts are triggered if the unit is not returned on time.

This functionality was observed to operate reliably, ensuring compliance with rental rules and minimizing misuse. The implementation of automated tracking and alerts enhances operational efficiency and reduces the need for manual monitoring.

\section {Network Encryption Security Feature}

To ensure secure communication between system components, network encryption mechanisms are implemented. These mechanisms protect sensitive data such as user information, transaction records, and telemetry data from unauthorized access.

The system demonstrated secure data transmission across connected devices, maintaining confidentiality and integrity. This security layer is essential in preventing data breaches and ensuring trust in the system’s digital infrastructure.

\subsubsection{Component Layout and System Arrangement}

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \caption{Solar-Powered Rental Station Internal Component Layout}
    \label{fig:3d}
    \includegraphics[width=1\textwidth]{figures/station-new-layout.png}
\end{figure}


The figure illustrates the updated structural and functional layout of the solar-powered rental station, which serves as the central unit for energy generation, storage, charging, and network distribution. The solar charger controller is mounted on the upper-left section of the enclosure to regulate the energy collected from the photovoltaic system and to protect the LiFePO4 battery from overcharging. Adjacent to it is the DC/AC breaker panel, which provides electrical protection and allows easier system control and maintenance. At the lower portion of the enclosure, the LiFePO4 battery is positioned to improve stability due to its weight and to support safer heat dissipation. Connected near the battery is the power inverter, which converts DC power into usable AC power for the station components. On the upper platform, two battery charger units are installed to support charging operations, while the coin slot mechanism positioned between them serves as the payment interface for users before accessing the charging service. Above the charging section, the LCD is mounted at eye level to display charging information, system status, and user instructions clearly and conveniently.

The control and communication components are arranged along the right-side compartment of the station to maintain an organized and efficient layout. The ESP32 microcontroller is installed to handle system monitoring, automation, and communication processes, while the buck converter located below it regulates and steps down voltage for low-power electronic devices. A WiFi router and MikroTik device are also integrated into the enclosure to provide internet connectivity and network management functions within the system. Additionally, extension wire outlets are positioned near the lower section to provide accessible external power connections for users. Overall, the updated arrangement follows a compact and layered design that separates the power, charging, control, communication, and user interface components, improving safety, accessibility, maintenance efficiency, and overall operational performance of the solar-powered rental station.

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \caption{Detachable Power Source Internal Component Layout}
    \label{fig:3d}
    \includegraphics[width=1\textwidth]{figures/portable-design.jpg}
\end{figure}


The figure presents the internal configuration of the portable detachable power source, which is designed to be compact, mobile, and reliable during use. At the center of the enclosure is the LiFePO4 battery, occupying the largest space to maintain balance and ensure stability when the unit is being transported. The remaining components are arranged along the inner walls to make efficient use of space while keeping the system organized.

On one side, the ESP32 microcontroller and voltage divider are installed to allow real-time monitoring of battery conditions while keeping the wiring simple and manageable. Directly below them, the buck converter regulates the voltage supplied to low-power components. On the opposite side, the relay module is positioned near the power inverter to control the output supplied to connected devices. The DC breaker is placed near the top section for quick disconnection in case of faults, adding an extra layer of safety. A compact Wi-Fi router is also included to support communication between the unit and the monitoring system.

The overall layout highlights a modular and accessible design, making it easier to maintain or replace components when needed. Proper spacing between heat-generating and sensitive parts is also considered to support better thermal management and extend component lifespan. In general, the portable unit demonstrates a well-organized internal structure that balances functionality, durability, and ease of use in actual operating conditions.

\section {Functional and Usability Testing Results for Community Residents}

Beyond evaluating the technical performance of the proposed solar-powered rental station with detachable power sources, it was equally important to assess how effectively the system functions in actual use and how well community residents can interact with it. To address this, functional and usability testing were conducted to evaluate the system’s core operations, responsiveness, ease of navigation, and overall user experience. The testing focused on essential functionalities such as user authentication, input validation, rental processing, system navigation, and interface interaction through the mobile application. In addition, usability aspects such as clarity of instructions, accessibility of features, responsiveness of outputs, and user understanding of system prompts were examined. These evaluations aimed to determine whether the system not only performs its intended functions correctly but also provides a seamless and user-friendly experience for community residents, particularly in scenarios involving power interruptions and emergency energy access.

Additionally, all required input fields were properly recognized and validated, ensuring that only complete and correct user data were processed. These findings confirm that the system’s functional components—including the mobile application interface, authentication mechanisms, and backend processing—are reliable and capable of supporting the intended user interactions.
