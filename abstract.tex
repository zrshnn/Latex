{\baselineskip=2.0\baselineskip

This study presents the design and development of KURYENTA: A Solar-Powered Rental Station with Detachable Power Sources, intended to provide an alternative energy solution for communities experiencing frequent power interruptions, particularly in Barangay Tupsan, Mambajao, Camiguin. The system integrates photovoltaic (PV) solar panels, LiFePO₄ battery storage, and a pure sine wave inverter to supply reliable AC power for small-to-medium household appliances.

To enhance accessibility and usability, the system employs a coin-operated mechanism and a mobile application that enables users to rent detachable power units, monitor battery status, and manage transactions. The integration of an ESP32 microcontroller with Internet of Things (IoT) technology allows real-time monitoring of battery performance, rental duration, and system status. Additionally, GPS and SMS notifier modules were incorporated to provide location tracking, security, and automated notifications for rental monitoring.

The developed prototype was evaluated through system testing, usability testing, and performance analysis. Results indicate that the system operates reliably, with stable power output, accurate monitoring, and effective communication features. Usability testing further confirmed that the system is user-friendly and suitable for community deployment. The findings demonstrate that the proposed system is a feasible, low-cost, and sustainable alternative to conventional backup power solutions, particularly for users who cannot afford high upfront installation costs.

Overall, the study highlights the potential of integrating renewable energy, IoT, and rental-based access models to improve energy accessibility, promote sustainability, and support community resilience during power interruptions.
